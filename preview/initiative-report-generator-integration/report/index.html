About this data

Land cover inside your area comes from the National Land Cover Database 2021 (Multi-Resolution Land Characteristics Consortium), read as a rendered image of your area from MRLC’s map service, clipped to your boundary and tallied by class colour. The sixteen NLCD classes are grouped as forest (deciduous, evergreen, mixed), cropland, pasture/hay, developed (all four intensities), wetlands (woody and emergent), open water, shrub and grassland, and barren/other. Acres are the pixel count scaled for latitude; the image is rendered at about 1,000 pixels across your area, so very small areas are read at NLCD’s native 30 m and large areas at a coarser scale noted under the bar.

About this data

This species list is created by combining the threatened species list for each watershed that your selection crosses. It is a dataset created by filtering the NatureServe at-risk species data. Southern Waters filtered by conservation status and has included species that are considered imperiled and/or critically imperiled on a sub-national or global level (G1, G2, G3, S1, S2, S3). A summary of all species included in this dataset can be found here. Generic placeholder entries in the source lists (names beginning with a lowercase article, such as “a cavesnail”) are left out, so counts here can be one lower than the published layer’s for a few watersheds. We have also indicated whether or not those species are listed as Southeast Regional Species of Greatest Conservation Need (SE RSGCN) species using this data.

About this data

Organizations come from the Landscape Partnership organization directory (service), a curated list of conservation partners across the Southeast. Each organization is placed at its headquarters or office address, so this section lists organizations located inside your boundary and, by default, within 50 miles of it — an organization's service area is usually much larger than its office, so treat the nearby list as outreach leads rather than a complete map of who works in your area.

Agency offices come from three AquaCorridors layers: USFWS Partners for Fish and Wildlife staff, USFWS Coastal Program staff and USDA NRCS state offices. Staff points are geocoded office addresses; the NRCS state office for each state your boundary touches is always listed, however far away it sits. Some directory addresses were filled in by an AI-assisted lookup (mostly from SAM.gov registrations); those records show a confidence note in the explorer.

About this data

Priority area boundaries are the polygon layers in the Priority Area Layers group of the AquaCorridors Mapper web map: U.S. Forest Service priority watersheds, SARP Conservation Opportunity Areas, USDA NRCS Aquatic Connectivity Framework priority areas, USDA NWQI planning watersheds, USFWS Partners for Fish and Wildlife and Coastal Program focus areas, and Sentinel Landscapes. Each partner publishes and maintains its own layer; the organization details in the table are written by Southern Waters to describe them.

Coverage is computed by intersecting each layer's features with your boundary. Layers that overlap your area are switched on by default and drawn in full around it (strong fills touch your boundary, light fills are the rest of the layer nearby); layers with nothing inside are switched off. The toggles above the table turn any layer on or off, and the map, the table and the explorer follow. Layers that require an ArcGIS sign-in cannot be read here and are marked as such.

About this data

The Southeast Conservation Blueprint is a living, spatial plan to achieve the SECAS vision of a connected network of lands and waters across the Southeast and Caribbean. It identifies priority areas based on a suite of natural and cultural resource indicators representing terrestrial, freshwater, and marine ecosystems. A connectivity analysis identifies corridors that link coastal and inland areas and span climate gradients. So far, more than 2,000 people from over 600 organizations have actively participated in developing the Blueprint. Source: secassoutheast.org/blueprint.

The Blueprint 2025 is a 30-metre raster. Each cell is ranked highest, high or medium priority for the connected network, or flagged as a priority connection (a corridor); everything else is lower priority. This report reads it from the SECAS Blueprint 2025 image service.

Acres are computed here in the browser by overlaying your boundary on the Blueprint raster tiles and summing the cells of each class, corrected for map-projection scale at your latitude. Percentages are shares of the area inside your boundary.

Note. The analysis, maps, and data on this site are for conservation planning purposes and are not for regulatory decisions. Data should not be transferred to a third party, in data or map form, without noting these disclaimers and providing the accompanying metadata.

About this data

This data is from the EPA's ATTAINS dataset (Assessment, Total Maximum Daily Load Tracking and Implementation System), which provides a comprehensive view of water quality assessments and TMDL data across the United States. It tracks impaired waters — those that fail to meet water quality standards — and documents state-reported efforts to restore these waters through TMDLs. Source: EPA, read live from the ATTAINS Assessment map service.

States and tribes draw their assessment units differently — most report streams as lines and lakes as polygons, but some report lakes, springs or beaches as points — so this report queries all three ATTAINS geometry layers and combines them by assessment unit. Each water appears once in the table no matter how many map segments it has. Impaired stream miles and lake acres are the portions of impaired units that fall inside your boundary. Each unit carries its organization's most recent reporting cycle, so cycles can differ between states.

Explore more with the EPA's How's My Waterway tool.

About this data

The U.S. Drought Monitor (USDM) is produced weekly by the National Drought Mitigation Center, USDA and NOAA. It classifies drought intensity from D0 (abnormally dry) through D1 moderate, D2 severe, D3 extreme and D4 exceptional drought, blending precipitation, soil moisture, streamflow and local expert input. This report reads Esri's USA Drought Intensity 2000 – Present live feed (service), updated every Thursday.

Current acres and the history both come from the USDM's weekly county statistics: for every county touching your boundary, the share of the county in each class is weighted by the acres of that county inside your boundary ("D1+" means moderate drought or worse). The map draws the USDM polygons themselves. "In drought" in the history means at least half of your area was in D1 or worse that week.

Streamflow comes from USGS gages inside (or, when none, nearest to) your boundary: the latest discharge from the USGS Water Data OGC API compared with that gage’s daily percentiles for today’s date (NWIS daily statistics), using the WaterWatch classes: much below normal (under the 10th percentile), below (10–25th), normal (25–75th), above (75–90th), much above (over the 90th). Gages without a current reading or a long enough record are skipped. The drought outlook is NOAA’s Climate Prediction Center seasonal and monthly drought outlooks (service), clipped to your boundary and reported as the share of your area under each outlook category.

About this data

County-level results of USDA’s 2022 Census of Agriculture (National Agricultural Statistics Service), as published in the Ag Census Web Maps dataset and joined to the counties of the Southern Waters programmatic boundary. Variables: number of farms; land in farms and total cropland as a percent of county land area; irrigated land, harvested cropland and pastureland as a percent of land in farms; farms by value of sales; net cash farm income of operations and of producers (total and average per farm); and total income from farm-related sources per farm.

The census is reported by county, not by watershed or drawn area. Each county that intersects your boundary is weighted by the share of its area that falls inside: counts and dollar totals are multiplied by that share, and percentages are averaged across counties weighted by the land inside your boundary (sales-class percentages are weighted by farms). A small area therefore inherits the values of the county or counties around it. Values NASS withholds to avoid disclosing individual operations appear as “—”. Counties whose overlap with your area is under 100 acres are left out.
